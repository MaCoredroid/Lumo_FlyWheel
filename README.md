# Lumo FlyWheel — gh-pages

This branch is published to GitHub Pages at
**<https://macoredroid.github.io/Lumo_FlyWheel/>**.

It contains self-contained HTML mini-papers:

- [`index.html`](index.html): Track-B Round-4b speculative-decoding ablation.
- [`round-f.html`](round-f.html): Volume II, the Round-F / Gated-DeltaNet reversal.
- [`gdn-tree-scan.html`](gdn-tree-scan.html): Volume III preview for the lossless GDN tree-scan paper.
- [`gdn-prefix-cache.html`](gdn-prefix-cache.html): Volume IV, bit-exact prefix caching (APC / EXACT_SEED) under speculative decoding.
- [`stateless-tree.html`](stateless-tree.html): Volume V, stateless tree speculative decoding — a branched verifier that caches and commits like native, plus the committed-token attention-KV garble fix.
- [`spine-reorder.html`](spine-reorder.html): Volume VI, one bit in the last place — why a wider speculative tree accepted fewer tokens (a superset violation), traced to online-softmax reduction reassociation (1 bf16 ULP) in the forked attention kernel, and the contiguous-spine reorder that restores the superset. Includes the MTP + Arctic suffix-decoding merge as future work.
- [`keep-or-replay.html`](keep-or-replay.html): Volume VII, keep or replay — why the branched tree out-accepts native linear MTP and still loses on throughput. The committer asymmetry (native keeps its recurrent states; the tree replays the accepted path through the 48-layer GDN scan on the critical path), the per-component decomposition (committer ~78% of the gap, drafter equal), the committer optimization ladder (burn redundant, replay isolated to ~16ms), the break-even model, and the honest native-wins verdict on the depth-matched 3-arm run.
- [`every-lever.html`](every-lever.html): Volume VIII, every lever we pulled — a full speed-campaign inventory: 16 attempts to make the branched tree faster, reported with the observation that motivated each, its mechanism, its measured effect, and the gate that decided it. 8 paid (truncated draft head 94.9->56.3ms/step, drafter graph capture, batched committer 47->36ms, subtree-parallel scan -22.3ms/event, ring staging 32.85->38.01 tok/s); 8 did not (whole-region graph capture: 35 boots, below the detection floor; the overlap class: dead at deployment, GPU 97% busy). Together they killed the ~140ms/event row tax, leaving a purely fixed ~85-90ms/step deficit.
- [`where-the-milliseconds-went.html`](where-the-milliseconds-went.html): Volume IX, where every millisecond went — a full accounting of the 232.780 ms/step batch-one decode step against the 119.658 ms mandatory-weight floor. What the campaign bought (step ~342 → 232.8 ms/step, drafter −61%, quality back to 9–11/16 resolved with zero give-ups, deployment batch 1.3 → 3.20), then the Nsight accounting: the target GEMM is 48.4% of the envelope with **no legal scheduler lever** (0.009 ms/step total, no wave-quantization penalty, 85.8% of the 273 GB/s roofline); the recoverable time is a 10.08 ms host-idle tail, 3.45 ms of scan imbalance, and 17.03 ms of attention headroom behind the exact-math wall. Full legal ladder = 19.4 ms/step against a 95.2 ms gap — it does not close, and that is the deliverable. Plus the composed six-kernel stack measuring null (1.0016×), the batch-four promotion criterion (a candidate posting +17.2% aggregate while every request got 2.96% slower), and Tier B: a second kernel-admission tier for candidates byte-impossible by construction, with a hard AST-pinned sampler invariant.
- [`numbers-that-didnt-survive.html`](numbers-that-didnt-survive.html): Volume X and the series close-out, twenty-seven milliseconds, shipped — **two speedups at the four-request deployment point**. Shipped and credentialed: attention query-head pairing, **−27.03 ms/step (408.3 → 381.3, −6.6%)**, sealed over four paired passes with a one-sided 95% lower bound of +10.82 ms, byte-identical, now the B4 production default (also flipped as the B1 default). Built and byte-proven across 11,088 comparisons, awaiting its seal: a single-pass recurrent scan, **−8.98 ms/step**. Both had measured ~null at one request — they attack *per-user* work, and the shared 27 GB weight read means the big matrix multiplies grow only 1.06× from one request to four while attention grows 3.26× and the recurrent scan 3.32×. Test at the width you deploy at. That prompted re-deriving the four biggest items on Vol. IX's own ladder: **three were not there** — a 10.08 ms host tail that was a mean over a bimodal population (median 3.59 ms) and a precondition rather than deferrable work; a 3.45 ms scan fusion priced by a model dividing real time by padded slots that never execute, against a schedule already critical-path-optimal; a conv brief costed against a pre-fix number (real: 0.542 ms, 0.2%). Plus a published 1.945 ms figure that was a **617× profiler artifact** (real: 3.15 µs). And the yardstick: the floors were wrong in three directions, so honest ratios are **1.833× (B1) and 2.574× (B4)** — and *per user* the four-request point is **1.81× closer to the floor** (58.3 ms excess vs 105.6). Also: an unnamed 83.9 ms/step kernel family larger than attention; attention shown **not** KV-bandwidth-bound at width 4 (marginal token ≤0.32 ns vs a 15.01 ns DRAM floor) but load-imbalance-bound; a per-width placebo design; a meter that recorded nothing for months while its audit passed; and 11 of 16 eval tasks shown to be constants. Written to be read without the previous volumes.
- [`only-quantization.html`](only-quantization.html): Volume XI and the opening of a new campaign, the port that was only quantization — moving the whole speculative-decoding stack onto a new model generation (Qwen 3.8-27B) and onto **four-bit weights (NVFP4)** on a single GB10. The architecture did not change: the config diff between the two model generations is **exactly one non-architectural line**, the tokenizer is byte-identical (so the drafter's tables carried free), and the whole risk lived in the four-bit compression. The four-bit model served coherent text (17×23=391 unprompted) and **kept its acceptance** (5.00 tokens/step mid-run, 4.526/event vs ~4.33 — a small gain, not a loss). What we measured, all at one request (B1): a split-K attention kernel took the step **under 200 ms for the first time** (214.759 → 196.423 ms, **−16.23 ms** on the deciding GPU-forward instrument, +18.15% throughput) — the first prediction in the campaign to beat its brief; the **four-bit output head over-delivered its floor budget** (−7.37 ms measured vs a 6.695 ms credit, zero top-choice flips over 792 rows); a fused draft kernel held **byte-exact over 96,215 steps** and shipped for hygiene (−0.071 ms); a composed two-lever stack sealed cleanly at **210.700 ms/step, 2.262× its floor** (n=4). Two floors were re-derived from scratch (conservative 102.480 ms, aggressive 92.345 ms — the four-bit head is nearly the whole gap). Honest about what it did **not** reach: **B4 was never served** (only micro-benchmarks), no fully-composed B1 headline was banked, and the sixteen-task quality read (**9 of 16 resolved**, one degenerated, one flag on the right task) is a *read* against a model-bound band, not a graded pass. Plus a discipline beat: a network-enabled agent was caught fetching its own gold patch, closed by cutting egress at the network-namespace layer. The campaign paused here, deliberately, with the levers banked. Written to be read without the previous volumes.
