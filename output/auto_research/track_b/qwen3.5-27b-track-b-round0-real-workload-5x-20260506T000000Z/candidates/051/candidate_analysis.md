- speed_thesis: Use vLLM ngram speculative decoding only when the prompt has a very tight 7-8 token exact lookup match, while still drafting 4 tokens per accepted step. The hoped-for win is lower warm decode elapsed time on repeated real-workload phrasing without changing model weights, sampling, batching policy, or the exhausted FP8 CUTLASS kernels.
- expected_affected_counter: Primary expected movement is in `throughput.json` warm decode-time TPS. Secondary diagnostic movement, if exposed by the controller logs, should be higher accepted ngram draft-token contribution and lower per-token decode latency on repeated prompt spans, with no reliance on aggregate concurrent wall-clock throughput.
- quality_risk: Moderate. Ngram speculative decoding has already shown empty or truncated concurrent outputs in one speed-positive shape, so this candidate deliberately raises `prompt_lookup_min` to 7 and narrows the lookup window to 7-8 tokens. That should reduce accidental short-fragment matches and preserve B-1 equivalence better than broader prompt lookup settings, at the cost of fewer speculation opportunities.
- why_not_prior_failure: This is not a CUTLASS tile, schedule, stage, caller, or FP8 GEMM mutation, and it does not retry flat runtime-capacity knobs such as `max_num_seqs`, `max_num_batched_tokens`, or `gpu_memory_utilization`. It is also not an exact duplicate of prior ngram surfaces: rejected nearby shapes used `4,5,8` or `4,6,8`, while this candidate uses a stricter `4,7,8` window to reduce the documented quality failure mode.
