- speed_thesis: Use vLLM ngram speculative decoding with a 4-token draft budget and a stricter 5-8 token prompt-lookup window. Prior ngram surfaces are the only serving-side changes that have materially exceeded the baseline-level launch-capacity and kernel-selection attempts; this shape keeps a nontrivial draft depth while requiring longer exact prompt matches before spending speculative work.
- expected_affected_counter: The primary counter is warm decode TPS from `throughput.json` over the real workload window. Secondary counters that should explain the result are speculative accepted-token rate, warm completion latency, and B-1 empty or truncated concurrent-output failures.

Ngram speculation is verifier-bounded by the target model, but prior broader or shorter-minimum lookup settings produced empty or truncated outputs under concurrent measurement. Raising `prompt_lookup_min` to 5 filters short repeated fragments and should reduce accidental draft continuation risk compared with min=2 and min=4 high-depth settings, at the cost of fewer accepted draft spans.
- why_not_prior_failure: This is not a CUTLASS tile, schedule, stage, or caller mutation, so it avoids the prior bandwidth-bound negative memory. It is also not another request-shaping, capacity-only, default-runtime, or duplicate kernel-selection surface. Compared with candidate 042's `5/4/8` ngram shape, this reduces speculative depth by one token and raises the minimum lookup length; compared with candidate 021's `4/4/8`, it keeps the draft budget but filters the shorter 4-token matches most likely to contribute to B-1 truncation risk.
