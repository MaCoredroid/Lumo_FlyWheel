- speed_thesis: Use vLLM ngram speculative decoding with a conservative two-token draft budget and a stricter three-token prompt match floor. Candidate 020 showed that ngram speculation can produce the only material warm-decode lift seen so far, while candidate 025 showed that two-token speculation with a 16-token lookup window can remain measurable and faster than baseline. Raising `prompt_lookup_min` from 2 to 3 is intended to filter ambiguous short matches that may contribute to empty or truncated concurrent outputs, while keeping `prompt_lookup_max: 16` to retain useful repeated-span opportunities in the warm workload.
- expected_affected_counter: Warm counted decode tok/s should be the primary affected counter. Secondary counters to inspect are accepted speculative tokens per target step, rejected ngram proposals, warm completion latency, and B-1 invalid/empty/truncated output counts.
- quality_risk: Medium-low. The target model weights, FP8 kernels, and sampling behavior are unchanged, and ngram speculative tokens are verified by the target model before acceptance. The remaining risk is serving-path behavior under concurrent warm completions; this candidate reduces that risk versus candidate 020 by limiting each speculative advance to two tokens and requiring at least a three-token exact prompt match before drafting.
- why_not_prior_failure: This is not a CUTLASS tile, schedule, stage, caller, or kernel mutation, so it avoids the prior bandwidth-bound CUTLASS negative memory. It is not request-shaping-only and not a flat vLLM launch-capacity override. It also avoids the exhausted `num_speculative_tokens: 3, prompt_lookup_min: 2` family and does not repeat any prior exact serving surface; compared with candidate 025, it changes the speculative-depth/minimum pair by raising the minimum lookup from 2 to 3 specifically to reduce B-1 truncation risk.
