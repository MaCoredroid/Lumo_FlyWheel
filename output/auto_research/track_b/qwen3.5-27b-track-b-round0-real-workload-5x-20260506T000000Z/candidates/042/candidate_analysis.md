- speed_thesis: Use vLLM ngram speculative decoding with a 5-token draft depth and a narrow 4-8 token prompt-lookup window. The higher speculative depth is the only recently observed serving lever with a chance of moving decode-time warm TPS materially above the flat launch-capacity and kernel-selection surfaces, while the 4-token minimum avoids spending draft work on very short repeated fragments.
- expected_affected_counter: Warm decode `throughput.json` TPS should improve if real-workload prompts contain repeated 4-8 token spans that let accepted ngram drafts skip target-model decode steps. The most relevant diagnostic counters are speculative accepted-token rate, decode-time warm TPS, and any B-1 empty/truncated output failures.
- quality_risk: Moderate. Ngram speculation should be semantics-preserving when accepted by the target verifier, but prior aggressive lookup settings produced empty or truncated concurrent outputs. Raising `prompt_lookup_min` to 4 narrows matches to longer prompt substrings and should reduce accidental draft continuations compared with min=2 settings, at the cost of fewer speculative opportunities.
- why_not_prior_failure: This does not repeat CUTLASS tile, schedule, stage, or caller mutations, which are negative memory for this objective. It is also not another request-shaping, capacity-only, or default-runtime config. Compared with failed `num_speculative_tokens=5, prompt_lookup_min=2, prompt_lookup_max=8`, this candidate keeps the same possible draft depth but removes the shortest lookup matches that were most likely to create unstable or low-quality speculative behavior.
