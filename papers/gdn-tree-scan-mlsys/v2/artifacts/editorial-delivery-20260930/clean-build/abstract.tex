\begin{abstract}
Tree speculative decoding for hybrid language models must preserve one coherent continuation across recurrent, convolution, and attention state. We present LumoTree, a verifier that executes recurrent paths in parallel, reuses state tiles within each path, and coordinates native recurrent replay, convolution-history gathering, and attention-cache remapping through a shared logical tree. The implementation combines fused candidate selection, GPU-resident acceptance, and grouped split-K attention. Component experiments establish candidate-selection parity and recurrent agreement within specified paired error bounds. Full-model correctness remains unestablished: across independent processes, the native reference preserves greedy decisions on all 84 tested paths but reproduces joint target and ordered draft selections on only 68. Exploratory Qwen3.8-27B NVFP4 deployments on GB10 attain 27.27--29.09 pooled tokens/s across three LumoTree builds, compared with 26.89 for SGLang EAGLE, on two shared SWE-bench Verified Astropy tasks with nonempty patches. These retrospective observations characterize the recorded deployments; they do not establish a same-stack speed advantage or general task-quality preservation.
\end{abstract}
