\begin{abstract}
Tree speculative decoding for hybrid language models must preserve one coherent continuation across recurrent, convolution, and attention state. We present LumoTree, a verifier that executes recurrent paths in parallel and coordinates native recurrent replay, convolution-history gathering, and attention-cache remapping through a shared logical tree. Fused candidate selection, GPU-resident acceptance, and grouped split-K attention support the verification cycle. On Qwen3.8-27B NVFP4, 126 continuation cycles satisfy frozen numerical bounds relative to native controls, with all ten injected faults detected. On a single NVIDIA DGX Spark (GB10), two disjoint 43-request corpora record 28.79 versus 26.12 and 30.74 versus 27.52 pooled tokens/s against five-token native multi-token prediction, with three runs per arm. A separate 30-attempt coding-agent study on ten SWE-bench Verified tasks does not reproduce this decode-rate ranking. Exact sampling is claimed only for penalty-free settings with matching probability filters, conditional on correct target probabilities. The deployed and coding-agent runs use presence penalty 1.0; incorrect tree histories make their sampling approximate after the first token of each step. These results characterize the implemented system's replay rates and bounded numerical agreement, without establishing lossless deployed sampling or task-completion speedup.
\end{abstract}
