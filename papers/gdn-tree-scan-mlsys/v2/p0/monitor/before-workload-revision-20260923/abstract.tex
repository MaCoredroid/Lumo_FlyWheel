\begin{abstract}
Tree speculative decoding for recurrent-hybrid language models requires each candidate to inherit its ancestors' state and each accepted path to become a valid continuation. We present GDN Tree-Scan, a Gated DeltaNet verifier organized around a shared descriptor, branch-local computation, device probability arithmetic, and selected-path publication across recurrent, convolution, and attention state. Its GPU execution uses temporary branch states and accepted-path replay through a shared node-update body. We explain memory--recomputation tradeoffs and the route constraints of implemented optimizations. Fresh same-input experiments compare sequential scan/replay with local triangular-solve and finite-Neumann realizations; neither compact candidate passes all final frozen promotion criteria. Bounded continuation checks qualify a synchronous stock-attention route. Eighteen fresh boots on GB10 compare fixed Qwen3.6-27B-FP8 configurations: the tree averages 12.69 versus native five-step multi-token prediction's 13.67 tokens/s at batch one, and 47.28 versus 55.38 at batch four, while exceeding the longer eleven-step control. These are three-boot means, not maximum tuned rates. Six additional batch-one boots evaluate reusing draft logits instead of recomputing the vocabulary projection for spine selection: the mean paired rate increase is 30.90\%, with a coarse 95\% percentile interval of $[29.81\%,32.90\%]$. Reuse was already active in the first comparison. Returned-token and wall-interval supports match, but cross-boot continuations differ. The results establish scoped numerical, state-management, and component-performance evidence; they do not establish equal-output acceleration, full-model equivalence, quality preservation, or a composed optimization gain.
\end{abstract}
