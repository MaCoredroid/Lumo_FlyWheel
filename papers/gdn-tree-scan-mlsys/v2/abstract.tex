\begin{abstract}
Tree speculative decoding for recurrent-hybrid language models requires each candidate to inherit its ancestors' state and each accepted path to become a valid continuation. We present GDN Tree-Scan, a Gated DeltaNet verifier organized around a shared tree descriptor, branch-local recurrent computation, device-side acceptance, and selected-path publication across recurrent, convolution, and attention state. Its GPU execution uses temporary branch states and accepted-path replay through a shared node-update body. We explain the resulting memory--recomputation tradeoff and implemented optimizations in draft-logit reuse, acceptance, convolution preparation, attention layout, and independent-work batching, distinguishing their route requirements from the configuration measured here. Fresh same-input experiments compare sequential scan/replay with local triangular-solve and finite-Neumann realizations; neither compact candidate passes all frozen promotion criteria. Bounded continuation checks qualify a synchronous stock-attention route. Eighteen fresh boots on GB10 compare instrumented Qwen3.6-27B-FP8 configurations: the tree averages 12.69 versus native five-step multi-token prediction's 13.67 tokens/s at batch one, and 47.28 versus 55.38 at batch four, while exceeding the longer eleven-step control. Returned tokens and wall intervals have matching support. Different attention backends, generated continuations, and engine seeds constrain these rates to the configurations as executed. The study contributes an integrated verifier design and scoped evidence for its numerical, state-management, and execution tradeoffs; it does not establish a general speedup, full-model equivalence, quality preservation, or gains from unmeasured optimization combinations.
\end{abstract}
