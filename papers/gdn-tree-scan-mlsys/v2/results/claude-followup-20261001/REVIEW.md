## Red-team findings

### Timing and memory claims

The new GDN totals include LumoTree's accepted native replay and Weaver's accepted replay. TreeWY's 11.0149-ms graph steady step fuses the previous publication with the current verification; the separately measured final deferred flush is **10.5098 ms**. A finite-session comparison must retain its first-step and last-step boundaries. Weaver's static-topology step excludes an approximately 0.02-ms graph topology rebuild, which is reported separately. These are isolated recurrent measurements, excluding attention, convolution, sampler, projections, and the other model layers. L2 flushing occurs between measured steps, not between each GDN instance. A claimed 6% full-model improvement from substituting Weaver is an unmeasured extrapolation and is not admitted.

The timing study treats numerical errors as descriptive; it does not enforce the original paired-error admission rules. TreeWY's author normalization floor and BF16 commit differ from the native convention. Weaver's author-default and FP32-preparation variants have markedly different state errors. Therefore these timings cannot establish equal-accuracy superiority or supersede the original M1 failures. The paper places the recorded errors next to the timings and retains the original failure table.

Named retained buffers are tensor-storage accounting, not total GPU memory: **132.5 MiB** for LumoTree, including a **96-MiB** export allocation shared across layers. Five cut rows logically export **15 MiB per layer**. That quantity is derived from shapes, not hardware traffic counters. The 1.5-GiB recurrent page allocation, outputs, graph pools, transient allocations, and common inputs are separate. The naive 5,040-MiB total includes a 34-slot bank (32 physical nodes, null, durable) plus staging; other methods use separate durable/page storage. These are method-specific allocation boundaries, not equal-footprint peak measurements. Zero additional graph-mode transient allocation does not mean zero retained buffers. Framework peak allocation records are retained separately; no system-memory reduction factor is claimed.

The SGLang token-ID timing starts at the first completion-count increase; vLLM starts at the first parsed nonempty content/reasoning/tool delta. Exact inputs alone do not make these timers identical. The new SGLang rate is a bounded cross-stack observation, not a controlled claim that one serving stack is faster or equivalent in distribution. Likewise, three disjoint run ranges are not a significance test or a per-optimization latency decomposition.

### Full-model protocol: outstanding admission concern

The new Q1 harness is frozen and committed but has completed **zero GPU cases or boots**. Its 33 schedules contain 147 consecutive commit cycles and cover eleven selected frontier patterns. This is selected-path coverage, not the original exhaustive 28-frontier × three-prefix (84-path) reference matrix. It also excludes prefix-cache lifecycle, native MTP/draft-state equivalence, and higher batching.

The strongest concern is the decision rule: all five surfaces have 0.02 floors; next-token KL tolerance additionally expands to four times the candidate's own cycle-zero verification error; the greedy band includes twice the candidate's cycle-zero maximum logit error, with a 0.25 floor. Native categorical disagreements can also exempt candidate cells. These choices are frozen before collection, but candidate-dependent calibration still absorbs verifier error. A pass is at most consistency of forced continuation under that envelope; it cannot by itself establish full verifier equivalence, exact greedy output match, or sampling preservation.

Before upgrading the scientific claim, separate a native-only full-model acceptance criterion from a continuation-only diagnostic that conditions on the verifier's measured error. Keep both outcomes and the original criteria. Negative controls should show sensitivity at the admitted error scale; detecting gross state corruption alone does not rule out smaller accumulated bias. Calibration/native validation failures must remain inconclusive rather than motivating post-outcome tolerance changes.

### Distribution and workload plan

The redesigned distribution protocol runs four AR boots, three tree boots, two MTP-5 boots, and one shifted-temperature control. It compares parsed-token marginal differences with the maximum of six AR-pair excess distances. This is a useful boot-variability diagnostic, but it has no stated equivalence margin or power guarantee and does not prove a joint output distribution. Native and candidate server seeds also differ. Failure to detect a difference must remain a diagnostic result, not proof of distribution preservation. KV-capacity variation is a hypothesis for the previous control failure, not an established cause.

The new SWE study needs effective budget/sampling/task-hash checks and completed patch evaluation in each arm. Equal wrapper wall limits are insufficient evidence of equal effective response caps. LumoTree's wrapper also enables GPU timers while the native wrapper differs; instrumentation overhead is not isolated. The queued native arm is MTP-5, not plain AR. One attempt per task permits a descriptive paired task report against these arms, not a benchmark-wide quality estimate. Current task counts must exclude server preparation, old deployments, and evaluations still pending.
