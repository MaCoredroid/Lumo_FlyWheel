## Required bounded revisions

1. **Correct the effect claim (proposal line 17).** Say the operation does not request content editing, truncation, unlinking, remapping or process changes; it can initiate pending file writeback and change kernel cache/metadata state. Do not claim “never changes file metadata,” “no I/O,” or that an old mtime proves the file has no dirty pages. Global Dirty/Writeback observations cannot prove that each target is clean. This is a targeted advisory cache operation with a possible writeback effect, not a pure read.

2. **Remove automatic repetition of the slab write (lines 33, 45).** The preceding recorded write freed 69.5637 GiB and left the partial accounting remainder small. The measured new obstruction is resident cache/buffers. Neither regrowth nor the assertion that memory “recovers only” through another manual slab operation is established. The smallest justified proposed attempt is three target advisories, post-residency observations and one query. If the parent chooses a compound treatment instead, authorize and record both operations explicitly under a new prospective policy; never inherit the consumed earlier write authority.

3. **Make full process visibility a precondition (lines 39–40).** `/proc` enumeration and maps/fd/fdinfo reads must report coverage and fail closed for permission denial, malformed data, inaccessible root-owned processes, or incomplete PID namespaces. An unprivileged scan cannot silently mean “no mappings/writers.” A separate, bounded privileged read-only inventory may satisfy visibility; it must not confer authority to perform the advisory as root. Distinguish a proved exited PID from an unreadable live PID, bind process identity across fd checks, and recheck immediately before the first operation. The campaign lock serializes owned operations, not arbitrary external processes; do not claim the scan eliminates all future races.

4. **Open once and retain verified descriptors (line 38).** Validate exactly three unique `(st_dev, st_ino)` values and approved paths; reject extra targets and duplicate hard-link aliases before any operation. Open read-only with no final-component symlink following, `fstat` all pinned identity/size/mtime_ns/ctime_ns fields, and retain those descriptors through the operation. Use their identities for process checks, mincore and fadvise; do not validate a pathname and later reopen it. Recheck metadata at the immediate boundary and after. Unmap the temporary mincore view before the no-mappings check/advice. Previously attested model bytes plus matching stat fields are the chosen continuity evidence, not a fresh cryptographic content proof.

5. **Bind operation start and uncertainty, not only successful returns (lines 45–54).** Durably consume the new authority before the first advisory; mark each inode attempted before entering its syscall. On error, interruption or deadline, preserve completed/attempted/not-attempted entries and stop; do not repeat a possibly completed operation. A timeout does not prove an in-kernel operation has ended. Retain the operational hold until the sole worker is confirmed terminal, and do not query/boot through an uncertain outstanding operation. A partial three-file treatment is a recorded failure, not permission to retry the remaining files. User-space attempts are bounded; kernel duration and freed bytes are not guaranteed.

6. **Prevent accidental cache repopulation and overstated comparability (lines 31, 38, 62).** Complete any target-content hashing before advisory calls. Keep source/manifest hashing distinct from reading the weight contents, and verify the caller does not rehash weights between eviction and the measured readiness boundary. Record residency and elapsed time; subsequent model loading naturally reads the weights. Replace “every arm boots cold ... under identical cache conditions” with **the same prospective treatment and recorded residency/admission observations**. Advisory success and even one zero-residency sample do not guarantee later identical cache state. This first candidate smoke does not establish a treatment for all future arms without their corresponding prospective policy.
