# Product

## Register

brand

## Users

The page serves two overlapping audiences: ML systems researchers who want a technically defensible preview of the lossless GDN tree-scan work, and portfolio readers who need to understand the scope, rigor, and engineering judgment behind the project without reading every report. Readers arrive from GitHub Pages or the repository and expect a concise but evidence-backed research artifact.

## Product Purpose

Lumo FlyWheel's Pages surface publishes mini-paper style previews of inference and benchmark work from the repository. The next page should preview the final paper on lossless GDN tree-scan: kernel design, attention-kernel work, wiring/debugging, lossless evidence, measurement infrastructure, speed optimization attempts, and current next steps. Success means the page reads like a serious research-lab blog post: clear enough for a technically literate recruiter, precise enough that an ML systems reviewer sees the proof obligations and evidence chain.

## Brand Personality

Rigorous, candid, and high-signal. The voice should feel like an engineering research note from a lab that red-teams its own wins: confident where the gates passed, explicit where deployment-binding evidence is still pending, and allergic to inflated hero numbers.

## Anti-references

Avoid generic portfolio landing pages, recruiter-first self-promotion, ungrounded "breakthrough" language, and decorative AI-site tropes. Do not replace the previous GitHub Pages artifacts. Do not hide unresolved gates behind a strong headline. Avoid a link-list page; the evidence trail should be synthesized into a readable argument.

## Design Principles

- Lead with the result, then show the proof ladder.
- Treat measurement infrastructure as a first-class contribution.
- Show failed and redirected attempts as scientific evidence, not clutter.
- Make the kernel path visually understandable before diving into equations.
- Preserve the mini-paper lineage while making Vol. III cleaner and easier to scan.

## Accessibility & Inclusion

Target WCAG AA contrast, keyboard-readable navigation, visible focus states, reduced-motion alternatives, and responsive layouts that preserve tables and diagrams on mobile. Charts and diagrams must include text equivalents or nearby explanatory captions.
